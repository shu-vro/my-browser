Gestures and zoom

Pinch to zoom
Pinch on the trackpad to magnify; double-tap with two fingers to zoom into what's under them (smart zoom).
Swipe between pages
Two fingers left or right on the trackpad go back or forward.
⌘ + scroll to zoom
Hold ⌘ and scroll (or swipe up and down on the trackpad) to change the page's zoom.
Default zoom
For sites you haven't zoomed. ⌘+ and ⌘- change one site's zoom, ⌘0 goes back; a button next to the address shows a site's own zoom.

Search keywords

Type a keyword, a space and what to search for, like w rust. Keywords go straight to the site; the search engine never sees them.

    Bangs

    Turn on the ones you want. Then type them with a !, before or after your search: !yt cats or cats !yt. All are off until you turn them on.

    Other

    Fast JavaScript (JIT)
    Off saves a lot of memory on heavy sites but makes JavaScript about 2–3× slower. Takes effect after restarting the browser.

    Tabs

    What each tab costs right now, as macOS counts it (the "Memory" column in Activity Monitor). A sleeping tab uses no memory and reloads when you click it. Tabs from the same site may share one page process.

    Keep background tabs awake
    At most this many; the ones you used least recently sleep, and links you open in the background beyond it load when you click them. 0 = no limit. Two in Low Power Mode. A background tab using 256 MB or more sleeps after three minutes, unless it's the last one you left (or tabs never sleep, in General). Pinned tabs, tabs playing sound or on a call, and tabs on screen in another window stay awake.

    Browser

      Privacy

      This browser sends nothing about you anywhere: no telemetry, no sync. History stays on this Mac. After a crash it asks whether to report it, and only sends what you choose to submit.

      Block trackers
      Blocks advertising, analytics, social and fingerprinting trackers (Disconnect's list, also used by Firefox) when they're loaded by other sites.
      Hide cookie banners
      Hides the consent pop-ups of common cookie platforms without answering them, so no consent is given. Sites with protections turned down show them.
      Block access to the local network
      Stops websites from reaching this Mac (localhost) and devices on your network such as routers and printers. Local pages themselves still work, and you can allow single sites from the site menu.
      Always on
      HTTPS first (plain HTTP only when a site has no HTTPS), Global Privacy Control, fingerprinting protection, tracking parameters removed from links (fbclid, gclid…), third-party cookies blocked, Intelligent Tracking Prevention, and no ad-click attribution. Turn protections down for one site from its site menu.
      on
      Fraudulent website warnings
      Warns about known phishing sites, by sending shortened URL fingerprints to Apple's Safe Browsing service. Off keeps every visit private.
      Clear cookies and site data
      Signs you out of every site and deletes caches and site storage.

      My filters

      Your own blocking rules, in uBlock Origin's and Adblock Plus's syntax: ##.ad hides matching elements everywhere, example.com##.banner on one site (and its subdomains), ~example.com##.x everywhere but there, ||ads.example^ blocks a server (add $third-party for other sites only). ! starts a comment. View → Hide Element on Page… adds a line for you. Sites with protections turned down are left alone.

      Site settings

      What each site may use. Sites ask the first time; your answer is remembered here. Change it below or from the site button left of the address bar.

        Bookmarks

        Bookmark a page with ⌘D or the star next to the address bar. They're also in the Bookmarks menu.

          Passwords

          When you sign in, the browser offers to save the password in your Mac's keychain (you'll find them in Keychain Access too). Click a login field later to pick a saved account; nothing is filled in without your choice, and only on the site it was saved for. Not offered in private windows.

          Ask for Touch ID
          Before filling or copying a password, confirm it's you with Touch ID or your Mac's password.

          From Apple's Passwords app, Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave or Firefox: export your passwords as a CSV file (Passwords app: File → Export All Passwords; Chrome: Settings → Passwords → Export), import it here, then delete the file.

            Autofill

            Click into an address or card field on a page to pick one of these; nothing is filled in until you pick. Card numbers are kept in your Mac's keychain and filled only on secure (HTTPS) pages, after Touch ID if that's on in Passwords. Security codes are never saved.

                      Uninstall

                      Deletes this browser and everything it keeps (logins, history, downloads list, extensions, settings), then quits. Downloaded files stay. This can't be undone.

                        Site data

                        What each website keeps on your Mac: sign-ins (cookies), saved settings (local storage and databases), offline copies and caches. Deleting a site's data signs you out of it. The site menu left of the address bar shows the same for the site you're on (Site Data…).

                          Extensions

                          Add Chrome extensions from the Chrome Web Store: open an extension's page there and click the puzzle-piece button next to the address bar. Or install a package (.crx or .zip) or an unpacked folder (with manifest.json). You'll see what it asks for first. Most Chrome extensions work; ones that need Chrome-only features may not.

                          Extensions are off on every site until you click their button in the toolbar there, and turn off again when you leave the site. Choose "On every site" for ones that must always run, like blockers. Right-click a button for the same choice.

                            Network

                            The browser itself connects to nothing on its own. When an extension needs the browser to connect for it (because WebKit can't), the request is blocked and listed here until you allow it. Requests you don't answer stay blocked, and this list is cleared when you quit.

                            "Allow address" covers that address whatever its query (?…) says; "Allow site" covers every address on that host. Allowed requests are remembered until you remove them.

                                Keyboard shortcuts

                                Every shortcut is a menu item. Click a shortcut, then press the new keys (with ⌘, ⌥ or ⌃); Esc cancels, Delete removes it. Changes apply at once and are kept in this profile. Shortcuts set in System Settings → Keyboard → App Shortcuts also work, and show here after a restart.

                                Profiles

                                Each profile is a separate browser: its own sign-ins, history, bookmarks, settings and extensions. A profile opens in its own copy of my-browser, with its name on the Dock icon and in the address bar. Also in File → Profiles. Saved passwords and cards are in your keychain, shared by every profile.

                                  Sync

                                  Move your browser to another Mac, or into another profile, with a file: your settings, bookmarks, history, search keywords, filters, addresses, site settings, zoom levels and shortcuts. my-browser never uploads it; you choose where it goes (AirDrop, iCloud Drive, a USB stick).

                                  Not in the file: passwords and cards (they stay in your keychain; use Passwords → Import on the other Mac with an export from Apple's Passwords app), cookies and sign-ins, extensions and downloads.

                                  Export
                                  Save everything above to a file.
                                  Import
                                  Add a file's bookmarks, history and the rest to this profile. What's already here is kept; its settings, keywords and site settings replace these.

                                  my-browser
